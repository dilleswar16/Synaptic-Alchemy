RFP Structure 

Split evaluation into two lanes:  
1) AIOps (event pipeline)  
2) AI SRE (investigation) 

Vendors are not directly comparable 

Run separate evaluation tracks instead of one unified score 

Vendor Comparison 

Avoid comparing all vendors together 

Different solutions solve different problems 

Map vendors to specific use cases (noise vs RCA vs workflow) 

Capability Focus 

AIOps solves alert noise; AI SRE solves root cause analysis; Incident tools solve workflow 

No single vendor covers all needs equally 

Prioritize based on primary pain point 

POC Approach 

Use real historical incidents with known outcomes 

Vendor demos are not reliable indicators 

Design a POC with actual MetLife scenarios 

POC Validation Metrics 

Measure accuracy, false positives, and RCA quality 

AI outputs can be misleading if not validated 

Define clear success criteria upfront 

Integration Evaluation 

Assess depth of integration (ServiceNow, Elastic, etc.) 

Integration complexity is often underestimated 

Require detailed integration effort + architecture clarity 

Data Readiness 

AI performance depends heavily on data quality 

Poor data will reduce effectiveness 

Validate CMDB, logs, and alert quality before POC 

Scalability Testing 

Test at enterprise-scale volumes 

Many vendors are not proven at scale 

Include production-scale scenarios in evaluation 

Vendor Maturity 

Vendors vary widely in funding, scale, and readiness 

Early-stage vendors carry a higher risk 

Include vendor viability scoring in the evaluation 

Pricing Transparency 

Pricing is inconsistent and largely unpublished 

Difficult to compare costs across vendors 

Request normalized pricing scenarios across vendors 

Cost Risk 

Usage-based models can create cost volatility 

Costs may increase unpredictably over time 

Negotiate caps, overage rates, and usage controls 

Discounting Reality 

Discounts are often erratic and misleading 

Discount % does not reflect true value 

Focus on unit economics and total cost, not discount % 

Commercial Leverage 

Vendors are competing for enterprise deals 

Buyers currently have strong leverage 

Push for aggressive pricing and favorable terms 

Contract Protections 

Address risks early (lock-in, usage growth) 

Hard to fix issues after deployment 

Secure exit terms, data portability, and rate protections upfront 

Consumption Control 

Define limits on usage-based pricing 

Avoid unexpected cost spikes 

Include consumption ceilings + true-down rights 

Rate Stability 

Lock pricing for multi-year deals 

Prices may increase post-deployment 

Negotiate multi-year rate lock (low/no uplift) 

POC-Based Contracting 

Tie agreements to POC success 

Reduces risk of underperformance 

Include POC success criteria in contract 

Security & Governance 

Require strict data usage and control measures 

AI introduces governance risks 

Ensure no data training + strong access control + auditability 

Exit Strategy 

Address knowledge lock-in risk 

AI learns environment over time 

Include data export + transition support clauses 

Vendor Strategy 

Include incumbents (ServiceNow, Splunk, etc.) 

Improves negotiation leverage 

Use as pricing and capability benchmark
