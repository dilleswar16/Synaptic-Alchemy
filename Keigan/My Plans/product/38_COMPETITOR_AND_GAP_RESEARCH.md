## 1. Summary in ten lines

1. Keigan is ahead of most of the market on honesty and traceability (honest outcomes, decision trace, per-answer tool trace, evidence-checked fixes, access-checked memory). It is behind on **what it can read** (no change records, no code, no CMDB), **where it can write** (ServiceNow gets only a state and close notes on resolve, and action-item tasks), and **how it lives in Teams** (a bot and rating buttons, no tab, no other card actions).
2. The biggest new finding for an enterprise ServiceNow shop: Keigan reads Azure's Activity Log for change but not **ServiceNow change requests**. Splunk's Event iQ Diagnose brings change context from ServiceNow and Jira into incidents ([Splunk ITSI](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/products/it-service-intelligence.html), read 2026-11-23). A "this change had no approved record" flag is something I did not find any vendor offering (own idea I-02).
3. incident.io's own documentation says its Microsoft Teams support is a bot that posts notifications, with buttons, forms and a tab, and no slash commands and no messages back to the bot ([docs](https://docs.incident.io/getting-started/teams-vs-slack), read 2026-11-23). Keigan's bot already answers questions in Teams, so on Teams Keigan is not behind that vendor. The gap is the tab and the card actions.
4. No competitor I read does everything in the Japanese UI and Japanese incident communications that Keigan does. ServiceNow lists Japanese as a second-priority language for its generative AI ([community guide](https://www.servicenow.com/community/now-assist-articles/multilingual-support-for-servicenow-generative-ai-products/ta-p/3099258), [S], 2026-11-23). I could not confirm that Keigan shows the RCA itself in Japanese (section 5, U-09).
5. On-call, paging and status pages are in every incident-management vendor's core and in none of Keigan's scope, by decision. I recommend keeping that decision. Opsgenie reaches end of support on 2027-04-05 ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/opsgenie/migration), [S], 2026-11-23): ask whether the target teams use it.
6. Cheap trust features that nobody I read ships: a confidence calibration chart, a blind-spot register built from "what Keigan could not check", and an investigability score per service (section 6).
7. Real-model trial and the evaluation harness remain the largest unknown (`00` section 3). Nothing in this document changes that: the next-10 list is for work that can proceed in parallel and does not need a model, except where marked.
8. Frontend work added from this list stacks on the existing browser-verification debt (`00` section 7, "Frontend verification debt"). Prefer backend-first items.
9. Six things I recommend **not** building: native mobile app, public status page, own on-call and paging, dashboard builder, plug-in or skill marketplace, any automatic remediation (section 7).
10. Open owner decisions are in section 9.
